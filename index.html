Batik tulis — about this game

The mechanic is the craft. Wax drawn with a canting seals the cloth. Whatever colour the fibre is holding at that moment is saved. Each dye bath darkens only the open cloth. When the wax is finally boiled away (nglorod), it disappears — and the colours it protected are what you see.

The real sequence this follows: nglowong (first wax outline) → medel (indigo bath) → mbironi (wax over the blue you want to keep) → nyoga (soga brown bath) → nglorod (the wax is removed).

Motifs. The shapes here are large, simple flowers, clouds, water, birds and stars drawn in the spirit of Javanese and Cirebon batik families. They are deliberately not reproductions of specific ceremonial patterns such as parang or kawung, which carry court and family meaning. Indonesian batik is on UNESCO's Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity (2009).

Safety. Real canting work uses wax at around 60–70°C. In this game the robot handles every hot tool; the child's finger only guides its hand. Nothing here can burn, spill or fail.

No timers, no score, no way to ruin the cloth.
